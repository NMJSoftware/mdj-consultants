What an importer MRN audit covers

A structured review of your HMRC Customs Declaration Service (CDS) data so you can see duty and import VAT paid, postponed amounts, possible refunds, correction cases, and clear next actions with deadlines.

  • Full CDS / declaration review
    Analysis of the declarations on file — duty and import VAT paid, volumes, and trends by month — so you have a clear picture of activity under your EORI.
  • Under and over payments
    Identification of potential refunds (overpaid duty or VAT) and declarations that may need correction or further review, with values quantified where possible.
  • Postponed VAT (PVA) check
    Review of import VAT that was postponed rather than paid at the border, and how it lines up with your VAT accounting.
  • Claims, corrections and deadlines
    Draft or open claims, correction cases, and the key action dates (including time limits for repayment claims) so nothing important is missed.
  • Written findings and actions
    A clear report covering overview metrics, what was found, prioritised actions (with urgency), and recommended next steps in plain English.

Who this is for

EORI-registered businesses that import goods into the UK and want comfort that their CDS data is clean — or that know there may be historic issues and want them quantified before HMRC does.

What we need from you

Access to (or extracts from) your CDS data for the relevant period, plus basic background on the goods and how you currently account for import VAT. We can guide you on how to obtain the data if needed.

Typical outputs

Overview of duty and import VAT paid vs postponed, potential refunds identified, flagged declarations, open or draft claims, prioritised actions with deadlines, and a clear view of import movements and any export issues that affect the position.

Why review CDS data now

  • HMRC is using the data

    CDS gives HMRC a detailed view of import activity. Errors that sit in the data can surface later as compliance checks, correction demands or repayment claims that work against you.

  • Overpayments are often left on the table

    Incorrect commodity codes, valuation issues or missed reliefs can mean duty or VAT has been paid that did not need to be. A structured review quantifies potential refunds and the declarations that support them.

  • Postponed VAT and timing risk

    Getting PVA wrong creates exposure on both the customs side and the VAT return. Time limits on corrections and repayment claims also mean delay can close off options permanently.
