If your letter has a deadline, treat it as real

HMRC deadlines usually run from the date on the letter, not the date you opened it. Missing one narrows your options and can increase penalties. Send it to us and we'll tell you where you stand.

What we deal with

If you're not sure which of these applies to you, that's normal — the letter often doesn't say plainly.

  • Compliance checks

    A routine enquiry into a return, a VAT position, or a specific figure. Handled properly and early, most close without escalating.

  • Code of Practice 8

    Serious enquiries where HMRC believes significant tax has been lost, but is not alleging fraud. The scope can widen quickly if it isn't managed.

  • Code of Practice 9 and the CDF

    Cases where HMRC suspects deliberate conduct. The window to respond is short and the decision matters — take advice before you accept or reject the offer.

  • Voluntary disclosures

    Coming forward before HMRC comes to you: let property, offshore income, undeclared trading, or historic errors. Disclosure made first almost always costs less.

  • Assessments, penalties and appeals

    Challenging a determination you believe is wrong, and arguing behaviour and penalty loading where the figures aren't in dispute but the label is.

  • Payment problems

    Where the liability is settled but the cash isn't there, negotiating time to pay on terms the business can actually survive.

How it runs

The aim is to take the correspondence off your desk and give you a clear view of the likely outcome as early as possible.

  1. Send us the letter

    Email a scan or a photo. We'll read it and come back to you, usually the same working day, with what it actually means.

  2. Free initial review

    A call to go through the background, the exposure as we see it, and the realistic range of outcomes. No obligation and nothing chargeable at this stage.

  3. Authority and fixed fee

    If you want us to act, we agree scope and fee in writing first, and file authority so HMRC deals with us instead of you.

  4. We run the case

    We prepare the analysis, answer the questions, and push back where HMRC has overreached — arguing the figures, the behaviour and the penalty position.

  5. Settlement and afterwards

    We agree the closing position, sort out payment terms if needed, and fix whatever caused it so it doesn't happen twice.
